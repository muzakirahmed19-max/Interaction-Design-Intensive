CClaude11 Nov, 16:52#8Wireframes and prototypes

Yes. These are Jakob Nielsen's 10 usability heuristics (Nielsen Norman Group). For each one I've added a line on what it could mean for the Project Overview screen.

  1. Visibility of system status: always keep users informed about what's going on, with timely feedback. Here: staffing status ("3 of 5 roles filled"), the clash count, a "today" marker, and when the data was last updated.
  2. Match between the system and the real world: use the user's words and familiar ideas, in a natural order. Here: the manager's own terms (weeks, hours per week, roles, deliverables), and a timeline that reads left to right like a calendar.
  3. User control and freedom: give a clear way out of mistakes or unwanted states. Here: easy ways back to Home or All projects, filters that are easy to clear, and zoom that can be reset.
  4. Consistency and standards: the same words and actions should mean the same thing everywhere, following platform conventions. Here: the same colours for clash, tentative, confirmed and unfilled as on every other screen, and Gantt conventions people already know.
  5. Error prevention: design so problems don't happen in the first place. Here: the Orion overlap is shown before he plans, and edit options are hidden on projects he doesn't manage.
  6. Recognition rather than recall: keep information and options visible, so users don't have to remember them. Here: a legend on screen, role and hours shown on each row, and a person's load across projects one step away.
  7. Flexibility and efficiency of use: support both new and expert users, with shortcuts for experts. Here: filters (only clashes, only open roles), grouping by role or phase, and zooming between weeks and months.
  8. Aesthetic and minimalist design: don't show anything that isn't needed, because every extra element competes with what matters. Here: the attention bar shows only what needs action, and details appear only when a row is selected.
  9. Help users recognize, diagnose and recover from errors: explain the problem in plain words and suggest a fix. Here: Er.11 says what failed and offers Retry or Reopen from Home, and each clash says how many hours over and which manager is involved.
  10. Help and documentation: it's best not to need it, but provide help in context when it's needed. Here: tooltips on markers and a short note on what "tentative" means, rather than a separate manual.

For this screen, 1, 5, 6 and 8 matter most, because it's dense and its job is to make the staffing state and clashes clear at a glance.

CClaude12 Nov, 11:52#13Wireframes and prototypes

Here are two pros and two cons for the Project Overview wireframe (Flexibility and efficiency of use):

Pros

  1. Experts move faster. Saved views, keyboard shortcuts (N, C, O, R, /) and bulk actions let a frequent user like Arjun reach a clash, an open role or the next step in one key press. He doesn't have to scan the whole timeline.
  2. Beginners aren't slowed down. Every shortcut is also a visible button or link, and the keys appear only as small hints. A first-time user can ignore them and still finish every task.

Cons

  1. The screen is dense. Saved views, filters, view controls, keys on the buttons and a shortcut strip all compete for attention. That can overwhelm a new user and make the main action, "Add staffing need", harder to spot.
  2. Shortcuts must be learned, and some skip steps. The speed gain only comes once keys are memorised. Jumps like "Staff role" and "Review clash" bypass the step where Arjun defines the staffing need, so he may miss checking the full requirement before choosing people.
