Identified Users and Key Tasks

Primary

Primary users

A

Arjun

Engineering Manager

Key tasks

  • Check who in his team and across other teams is free, partly free or fully booked
  • Allocate engineers to projects in hours per week, and edit, extend or remove allocations
  • Resolve conflicts when someone is overbooked
  • Monitor team workload and utilization, and spot overload or underuse early
  • Rebalance work when priorities or people change
N

Nisha

Project Manager

Key tasks

  • Find people by skill, proficiency, role and availability for a project
  • Request or allocate people to her project for a defined period
  • Forecast free capacity by role and skill for upcoming projects
  • Track her project's staffing and fill gaps when someone leaves or goes on leave
  • Agree effort in hours per week with managers and contributors
Secondary

Secondary users

IC

Individual Contributors

designers, developers, QA and DevOps

Key tasks

  • View their own allocations and total weekly commitments
  • Keep their skills, proficiency levels and availability up to date
  • Flag overload or raise concerns about their allocations
  • Check upcoming leave, which is synced from the timesheet system
DH

Delivery Head / Account Manager

Key tasks

  • Raise staffing requests for new or expanding client engagements
  • Check whether people with the right skills are available before committing start dates
  • Track the status of their requests until staffing is confirmed
AD

Admin / Resource Ops

Key tasks

  • Add, edit and deactivate people, roles and projects
  • Manage permissions for each user type
  • Maintain the skills list and effort standards
  • Monitor the timesheet leave sync and Slack integrations
Tertiary

Tertiary users

L

Leadership

CTO / COO, read-only

Key tasks

  • View organization-wide utilization, bench strength and capacity trends
  • Identify skill gaps and delivery risk for strategic decisions
F

Finance

read-only

Key tasks

  • View billable vs non-billable utilization
  • Use capacity and allocation data for revenue forecasting and project costing

Consolidated Scenario 1

Staffing a new project without double-booking

(Arjun – Primary)

Experiences covered

E1 Viewing availabilityE2 Finding people by skillE3 Allocating people to projectsE4 Managing conflictsE6 Monitoring team workload

Context

It's Monday morning. Arjun has to staff a new client project, "Atlas", which starts next Monday and needs 2 backend developers at 20 hrs/week for 6 weeks. His own project "Orion" is running a week past its deadline, and two of its developers are already booked on Atlas from tomorrow. Before the platform, he would check 3 spreadsheets and message other managers on Slack, which used to take about half a day.

Goal & Metrics

Goal: staff Atlas with the right people, without overbooking anyone or delaying Orion.

  • Time to staff a project: from approximately 4 hours down to under 30 minutes
  • Double-bookings confirmed: 0
  • No one in the team above 100% utilization after allocation
  • Number of Slack or meeting follow-ups needed to confirm availability: 0

Action

  1. Arjun opens the availability view and checks who in his team and other teams is free next week. Leave, public holidays and part-time contracts are already reflected, and he can see partial availability such as "12 of 40 hrs free" (S1, S3, S4, S11).
  2. Nobody with the backend role is fully free in his own team (S9), so he searches across the organization for "Node.js, Senior, 20 hrs/week free from next Monday" (S15). When a rarer skill returns no results, the platform suggests related skills (S24).
  3. He opens the profiles of the two best matches to check their skills, proficiency and past projects (S16).
  4. He allocates both at 20 hrs/week for 6 weeks (S29). He mistypes 200 instead of 20, and the platform stops him with a clear error (S43).
  5. The platform flags that Orion's overrun overlaps with two developers booked on Atlas from tomorrow (S46). He extends their Orion allocations by a week and sees who can and can't stay on (S48).
  6. A warning shows that one developer would reach 50 hrs/week (S51). Arjun opens the conflicts list (S52) and resolves the overlap by splitting her hours: 10 on Orion and 20 on Atlas for that week (S47, S53). He removes the second developer's Orion booking for that week, and the other manager involved is notified (S56, S57).
  7. Each person is notified of their new allocation (S33) and sees their updated weekly total in their own view (S92).
  8. Arjun checks the team utilization view. Nobody is above 100%, and one underused engineer can pick up Orion's remaining work, so he rebalances it to them (S80, S82).

User Emotion

  1. Start

    Stressed and unsure, expecting another afternoon of chasing people and fixing clashes.

  2. Middle

    Relieved when the overrun clash is caught before anyone is double-booked, and confident because the hours are clear and nothing is vague.

  3. End

    In control. Atlas is staffed in minutes, Orion is still covered, and nobody on the team is overloaded.

Consolidated Scenario 2

Keeping a slipping project on track

(Nisha – Primary)

Experiences covered

E1 Viewing availabilityE3 Allocating people to projectsE4 Managing conflictsE6 Monitoring team workload

Context

Nisha runs "Orion", a client project that has just slipped by 2 weeks because the client changed the scope. Some of her team are needed longer, some less, and one person isn't needed any more. She used to update 2 spreadsheets and ping 3 managers to make changes like these.

Goal & Metrics

Goal: re-plan the team for the new timeline without overloading anyone or leaving gaps.

  • Time to re-plan after a slip: from approximately 1 day down to under 1 hour
  • Allocation changes made without any back-and-forth with other managers: approximately 90%
  • Overlaps left unresolved after re-planning: 0

Action

  1. Nisha opens each team member's availability across all their projects for the next month (S2).
  2. She extends the allocations of the two developers she still needs by 2 weeks (S30).
  3. The designer's work is mostly done, so she reduces their hours from 30 to 15 per week (S31).
  4. QA is no longer needed after the new scope, so she removes the QA engineer from Orion (S32).
  5. One extension overlaps with a developer's next booking. She shifts the dates of his Orion allocation to avoid the overlap (S54).
  6. She checks the workload view for her project team only and confirms that nobody is above 100% (S84). Arjun sees that the QA engineer is now below 50% and available for other work (S81).

User Emotion

  1. Start

    Anxious, because the slip could cause a chain of clashes and difficult conversations.

  2. Middle

    Reassured, because every change shows its effect on hours straight away.

  3. End

    Confident. The new plan is clear, the overlap is resolved and nobody is overloaded.

Consolidated Scenario 3

Starting out and keeping my profile current

(Individual Contributor – Secondary)

Experiences covered

E11 Onboarding and first-time setupE7 Managing my profile and allocationsE1 Viewing availability

Context

Ravi, a backend developer, has just received his invite to the platform. He works for two managers and often isn't sure what he's committed to each week. He recently earned an AWS certification that nobody knows about.

Goal & Metrics

Goal: set up an accurate profile and always know his own commitments.

  • Profile completed on first login: within approximately 10 minutes
  • Skill profiles updated in the last 6 months: approximately 90% across the organization
  • Times he has to ask a manager "what am I on next week?": 0

Action

  1. Ravi logs in for the first time and completes his skill profile (S150).
  2. He adds AWS at an Intermediate level after his certification (S94), raises his Node.js level to Expert (S95) and removes jQuery, which he no longer uses (S96).
  3. He's moving to a 4-day week next month, so he updates his availability to 32 hrs/week (S97).
  4. He views his own availability for the next 4 weeks (S5) and his upcoming allocations, with project details and managers (S93).
  5. He sees the allocations from both of his managers together in one place (S103).

User Emotion

  1. Start

    Sceptical, expecting yet another tool to fill in.

  2. Middle

    Pleasantly surprised that setup is quick and his new skill is now visible to managers.

  3. End

    In control. He knows exactly what he's committed to, and his reduced hours will be respected.

Consolidated Scenario 4

Setting up the platform and retiring spreadsheets

(Admin – Secondary)

Experiences covered

E9 Setting up and administering the platformE11 Onboarding and first-time setup

Context

Meera, from Resource Ops, is launching the platform at Nimbus Labs. All the data is in managers' spreadsheets today, the skills list is messy and people keep joining and leaving.

Goal & Metrics

Goal: set up the platform with complete, clean data so that the spreadsheets can be retired.

  • People and projects set up in the platform: 100% within the first week
  • Spreadsheets still in use for resourcing: 0 within approximately 1 month
  • Import errors fixed before go-live: 100%

Action

  1. Meera imports people and allocations in bulk from the existing spreadsheets (S129). The platform flags duplicate rows and missing hours, and she fixes them before confirming (S132).
  2. She sets standard working hours at 40/week and records part-time contracts (S127).
  3. She assigns permissions for each role, such as managers, contributors and read-only for Leadership and Finance (S121).
  4. She adds missing skills to the skills list, e.g. "Figma" (S124).
  5. She creates the new project "Atlas" with its dates and manager (S122), and archives the completed project "Vega" (S123).
  6. She adds a new joiner with their role, team and permissions (S119), and moves another person to a new manager (S120).
  7. Someone who has left still has future allocations, so she deactivates them and their managers are alerted to reassign the work (S131).
  8. She sends invites to all users (S148).

User Emotion

  1. Start

    Overwhelmed by messy data from several sources.

  2. Middle

    Relieved that errors are caught before go-live instead of afterwards.

  3. End

    Proud. There is now one clean source of truth for the whole organization.
